Although this one is a little bit on the soft side it's really a good mantra.

There will come a time when you will be facing a decision straight in the face. 

Should you stride on by, or should you stop in your tracks?

This, in the programming way of life, is a constant dilemma. We are consistently forced to painstakingly establish priorities and abide by our decisions.

When we programmers are building - I'm generalizing here but bear with me - we want to do 100% of the shit we are doing, not just the 90%, not just the happy path, we want everything because we are fucking greedy.

That's the only way we feel happy. Haven't you ever felt totally depressed because it's Friday and you couldn't finish your refactor and the evening is coming, the natural light from the sun starts fading away and you keep going, just a little more, but now it's deep into the night, your family or your friends are calling, you are not finished and you know that you should stop. You shut down your computer and you go on with your life but, for everyone reading this, I just want to finish it and that's the only thing that will set me free.

However, that compelling urge to develop the most elegant solution known to humankind doesn't always "show up" when it comes to deeply understanding the problem in the first place.

If you have it, you pass, don't take more time reading this, but if you don't it's just one more paragraph.

Someday, at some specific moment you will have the feeling that you almost understand what is happening. You are not at 100% but you are brushing with it. You will think “this is enough”, but let me tell you that it’s not. If you turn the page this will come back to bite you in the butt. Maybe not today, maybe not tomorrow, but you will probably regret not going that extra mile to understand it fully.

Hope it helps.
